Research

November 2026 · Technical report · Software-defined radio

Receiving Sensus FlexNet Smart-Meter Transmissions with a Low-Cost SDR

Many electric utilities read their meters over Sensus FlexNet, a licensed-band radio network with no published receiver. Using a $30 RTL-SDR, I worked out how its uplink is modulated, built a receiver that recovers clean symbols, and mapped the frame layout. The payload behaves as ciphertext, consistent with the AES-256 encryption Sensus documents.

July 2015 · M.S. Information Security practicum · Intrusion detection

A System for Analyzing Historic Network Traffic with Current Rulesets

Signature-based intrusion detection only catches an attack once someone has written a rule for it, so anything exploited before disclosure goes unseen. Heartbleed went more than two years without a usable IDS signature. I built a system that pulls the latest Emerging Threats and Snort rulesets each day, runs them with Suricata over archived packet captures from Georgia Tech's campus network, and uses Splunk to flag alerts on traffic recorded before the matching signature existed. In a week of sampled traffic, one ET Pro signature fired on activity two days before it was published. This was an early take on what's now called retro-hunting, a standard feature of today's commercial security tools.
